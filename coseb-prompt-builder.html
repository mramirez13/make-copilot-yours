The prompt formula

A useful prompt makes five things clear.

Prompting Essentials teaches Goal, Context, Expectations, and Sources. This workshop uses COSEB: Objective is the goal, and Boundaries make the responsible-use expectations explicit.

C

Context

Situation + audience. Why does this request matter, and who will use the result?

O

Objective

The goal. What should Copilot help accomplish?

S

Sources

What information should ground the response? Name it; do not assume Copilot has it.

E

Expectations

What should the response look like? Specify format, tone, length, and quality.

B

Boundaries

What should it avoid, label, or leave for a person to review?

Basic prompt

Write an email about manager training.
Topic onlyNo audience or source

Stronger prompt

You are helping an HR business partner communicate with people managers about a new learning session. Explain why it matters, who should attend, and the next action. Use only supplied program information. Keep it under 175 words with a subject line. Use placeholders for missing facts. Do not invent mandatory attendance, policy, or leadership endorsements.
Audience + situationClear outcomeApproved sourceReviewable formatBoundaries + placeholders
The point is not to make every prompt longer. It is to make the work clear and grounded enough to review. A preference is not policy, and a polished response is not proof that its claims are correct.
